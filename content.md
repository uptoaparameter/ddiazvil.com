## Job Market Paper

**[Gini Index Regression](assets/papers/JMP_GIR_DiazVillarejo.pdf)** <br>
*with Vedant Bhardwaj*

> Many economic questions concern how and why inequality varies between populations with different characteristics. Comparisons of average outcomes do not reveal such variation. This paper develops Gini Index Regression (GIR), a framework in the spirit of mean or quantile regression that directly models the Gini of the conditional distribution of a nonnegative outcome, without choosing a parametric functional form for that distribution. In the linear case, the parameter of interest is shown to have a closed-form solution and, under model misspecification, the fitted function is a weighted best linear approximation to the conditional Gini. An omitted variable bias formula shows that changing controls affects both the projection and the ranking of outcomes. The estimator combines nonparametric series estimation of conditional ranks with weighted least squares, and reproduces empirical group Ginis in saturated group-indicator specifications. We establish √*n*-consistency, asymptotic normality, and validity of the bootstrap, accounting for both stages. A prediction characterization of the conditional Gini is also used to propose an $R^{2}$-type goodness-of-fit measure. An application to U.S. data finds that the wage inequality gap by union status remains significant even after controlling for relevant characteristics, and that college-educated workers indeed possess the lowest levels of inequality.

### Working Papers

**Locally Robust Semiparametric Estimation of Parametric Conditional Gini** <br>
*with Vedant Bhardwaj, Juan Carlos Escanciano, and Joel R. Terschuur*

> Using the influence function for functionals of conditional distributions, we construct GIR-based moments that are first-order insensitive to local misspecification of the first-stage conditional rank nuisance function. We then develop locally robust estimation and inference procedures for GIR. The current analysis considers three alternative and well-motivated GIR specifications, two of them nonlinear, and nonparametric kernel estimation of the conditional ranks. It provides a basis for investigating how inequality regressions can accommodate a wider range of flexible estimators, allowing for local first-stage misspecification but without sacrificing valid inference. Theoretical findings are illustrated via Monte Carlo experiments and an empirical application.

**A Durbin–FGLS Approach to Robust Inference for Time Series Regression**

> Although standard heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation (HAC) robust inference methods remain agnostic about the form of serial correlation in regression scores, they are known to fail in important cases. Conventional procedures that aim to learn the dynamics of regression errors (GLS) fail when strict exogeneity requirements are not satisfied. I propose a new feasible GLS estimator based on Durbin regressions that learns the relevant dynamics nonparametrically. The proposed approach allows for much weaker exogeneity conditions under which OLS or existing GLS variants are not consistent. The results cover √*n*-consistency and asymptotic normality, showing that the estimator is efficient whenever the GLS variants are. Theoretical findings are illustrated via Monte Carlo experiments.
